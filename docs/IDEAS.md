## Parked Ideas

### Flexible Price Expectations Through Third-Party Agents

Date: 2026-08-03

Status: parked hypothesis, not an active task.

Observation:

In Test 006, an external blind agent did not receive a contractor price list and did not invent a fixed contractor quote. Instead, it independently placed the offer into a broad competitive market context using external public sources, while keeping the contractor's actual price dependent on inspection and project specifics.

Why this matters:

- Missing fixed prices may be a useful flexibility point, not only a trust gap.
- A third-party agent can frame the contractor as a normal market supplier without the project itself making a hard price promise.
- This can help create early trust: the offer feels understandable, contactable, and commercially normal.
- In future sales experiments, the same flexibility could support different expectation levels depending on positioning, proof, quality signals, warranty, real references, and demand.

Important boundary:

This is an idea for future sales and expectation-management experiments. Do not change the current contract or pricing behavior only because of this observation. Test it only when there is a real practical task or a concrete sales technology to evaluate.

Current decision:

Leave the current price behavior as-is. Keep separating contractor estimates from market background, but do not add stricter price-range prohibitions yet.
